The BIS Annual Economic Report 2025 notes that a USD 3.5 bn stablecoin inflow can lower T-bill yields by around 2.5–5 bp. The GENIUS Act now requires that reserve Treasuries mature within 93 days. Has the link become stronger? Not so far.

Pre-registered local projections, daily data from Jan 2021 to Oct 2026, 3-month bill, per USD 10 bn five-day inflow:

1) Before the Act: −10.9 bp at day 10 (90% CI −17.7 to −4.1). After: −5.6 bp (−13.0 to +1.9). The difference, +5.3 bp (p = 0.30), has the opposite sign to my hypothesis.
2) The short-end concentration (4-week −14.8 bp vs 2-year ≈ 0) predates the Act.
3) The pre-Act outflow asymmetry (+16.9 vs −1.6 bp per USD 3.5 bn) is absent after it. 321 of 412 pre-Act outflow days fall in the 2022–23 hiking cycle.

The result I most want challenged: a placebo break in July 2024, with no legislation, is about four times larger (+23.1 bp, p < 0.01). Controls-only estimates of this channel look unstable over time, which strengthens the case for an instrument like the one Ahmed and Aldasoro use.

Limits: these are associations, not causal effects. The post-Act sample is short (detectable change ≈ 15 bp), and the reserve rules bind only from January 2027.

This builds on Rashad Ahmed and Iñaki Aldasoro, "Stablecoins and safe asset prices" (BIS WP 1270).
